As a kid, I remember we came up with a fun, stupid idea (as they all are). We were on top of some small hill, which from one side had a vertical wall, but on top of it there was soil.
We figured out we could grasp the grass and pull it with some soil. So we came up with a game where we would pull them and throw them at the cars.
The alarm of one of the cars went on and we ran, or at least my friend managed to run away. I was caught by the owner of the car and made to clean the front window.

A barrage of lightnings under the low deck of the supercell was raging but I felt totally safe taking pictures in the middle of the plowed field, as I knew, the devil doesn’t take evil people.

  

Shoving coal at grandmother’s old house on the last warm day of the fall, done too late. First few bucks made by my hands.

  

When I was a small kid, on summer my family would take me to the countryside, in a different part of the country, to spend the hot months at my grandparents house, in the village. Behind them, the hills. And many magic places: The Pit, where we used to sit and refresh and drink its cold, crystal clean water; the Old Castle, where we never set foot, because of the grim legend about it; and the Witch House, on the very top of the hill, abandonded, crumbling, surrounded by the wheat fields, and I remember a crisp cold wind was always blowing up there, no matter what day of the year or what time of the day.

# story

There was a man who was sitting in the playground’s sandbox. He was digging holes in the sand, almost as if he was a kid.
He was a director of a scene that was playing in the caves of the sand.

This was the only way he could stop thinking about all the real life problems. Becoming a child. Giving a meaning to mundane things.
He would come up with ridiculous worlds so he could stop himself of thinking of all miserable things he’s experiencing in real life.
But the things that bothered him in real life would transfer over to the stories. He was not in control, even when he thought he was.

Which is why one of his characters wrote a farewell letter, to his close friend, or all of them, if he even had such. To some of people he would be seeing recently. Just not to his parents, they didn’t deserve any words, he thought.
But he didn’t finish himself off yet, the character had to play a role in his script for some time, otherwise what’s the point of setting up such character? Just to kill him?
So the soon to be suicidilist, he was working hard labor in workplaces he hated. He hated the people, the job and the fact he had to work overtime because he was told to. He compared the work to a coal mine.

Another character was a perfect thief. And that also resembled our director in his world. He was thinking about stealing on everyday basis. Not that he was doing it, but was thinking about it. He had money, just he figured why would he pay anyone? Why does he ove them?
In fact, if he smoothly walks into the store, and grabs swiftly whatever small item he is after then walks out, he would save so much time on not having to stand in the queue to pay.

This director kept playing with his stories in the sandbox. No matter it was starting to rain and the thunder could be heard from a distance. He was lost in his worlds and he didn’t see much beyond them. In fact the environmental changes only nudged his stories forward. The mere rain meant the tunnels flooded. The lightnings were a random photographer on the street that was taking a picture of other people but forgot to turn the flash off.

He would spend days and days in the playground. Forgetting to eat or drink. 
Nevertheless 
One time someone called the police on him, reasoning he’s stealing kids’ space from them. He raised his head stared for a moment and said: “The prophecy was real, the civilization has failed it’s duty, for the giants have arrived to destroy them” as one of the officers carelessly stepped on one of the sand buildings, meaning it’s collapse.

Confused policemen had no clue what to do with the man. They assumed he’s crazy and there was nothing in the law prohibiting adults to the playgrounds, so they left him. 

And the man continued to create stories in the sandbox.
